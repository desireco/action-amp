# ActionAmp — Product

## Users
People with too much on their plate — the chronically overwhelmed, the
easily distracted, anyone who has ever stared at a 47-item todo list and
done nothing. We say "built for anyone overwhelmed" rather than naming a
specific condition. ADHD is the design muse, not the marketing target.

## Product Purpose
A focus app whose entire thesis is: **stop optimizing capture, start
optimizing the decision.** Every other todo app opens to a list. ActionAmp
opens to *one task* — the next thing that matters — and hides the rest.

The home screen isn't a list. It's a decision.

## Brand Register
**Brand.** The landing page is where the design IS the product — it has to
embody the calm, opinionated, "we are not like the others" stance in its own
composition. The app (separate work) is the product register where design
serves.

## Tone of Voice
- **Calm, not chirpy.** No exclamation marks, no "🚀 Let's go!" energy.
- **Direct, not clinical.** "You don't fail to capture. You fail to pick."
- **Opinionated, not aggressive.** We have a thesis; we state it without
  trashing competitors.
- **Honest, not salesy.** "Soon. Maybe one email before then." Admit
  uncertainty rather than fake momentum.

## Anti-References (do NOT look like these)
- **Todoist / TickTick / Asana / Things** — calm but they all open to a list.
  We open to a decision.
- **Linear** — beautiful but cold and enterprise. We are warm and personal.
- **Notion marketing** — pretty but wordy and chirpy. We are restrained.
- **Honeydue / streak apps** — guilt-trip red dots, badge counts,
  manipulation. Banned entirely.

## Strategic Principles (load-bearing — do not violate)
1. **The list is demoted.** Next (one task) is the home screen.
2. **Calm over features.** Whitespace is the point. If a section feels
   crowded, remove something.
3. **Fair to users.** Asking for the signup, selling the paid plan,
   sending the occasional nudge — none of that is dishonest; it's how a
   sustainable product treats people like adults. The commitment is
   fairness, not anti-commerce. What we won't do: **deceive** (fake
   scarcity, invented social proof, hidden charges, dark patterns),
   **trap** (hard-to-cancel, data lock-in, silent renewals), or
   **guilt-trip** (streaks-as-anxiety, red-dot nagging, badge FOMO).
   Real scarcity stated honestly — the Founding 100 cap — is fair;
   invented scarcity is not. The product competes on being worth it, not
   on making the alternatives painful.
4. **Methodology-aware but invisible.** GTD + PARA-flavored (Goals replace
   Areas) — credibility for power users, invisible to beginners.
5. **Keyboard-first, native feel.** System font, restrained motion, every
   action has a shortcut.
6. **Modal, not menu.** Navigation is a state (Plan/Do/Review × Task/Project/
   Goal), not a sidebar of nouns. (This is in the app; the landing page
   gestures at it but doesn't have to teach it.)

## Visual Identity (see `docs/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md` for ActionAmp's tokens; `DESIGN.md` for Things DNA)
- **Things DNA**: cool-tinted neutrals (never pure gray), soft blue-tinted
  layered shadows, 4–8px radii, generous whitespace, system font.
- **Two-accent system**: Teal (#0EA5E9) = system/state (completion,
  selection, CTA); Amber (#F59E0B) = human emphasis (rare — Important
  priority, "why this" nudges).
- **Brand mark**: teal rounded-square with white checkmark — the logo AND
  the completion state AND the favicon (one visual, three jobs).

## Current CTA

Three surfaces, all fair-play:

- **Signup** — the hero CTA ("Make an account") and the final CTA both send
  ready visitors to `/signup`. Asking for the signup is not manipulation; it's
  the point.
- **Newsletter** — a plain email-capture field in the footer (always) and a
  quiet one in the hero (alongside the signup button). One line: *"One email
  when there's something to say."* No invented urgency, but no apology either.
- **The Founding 100** (`/founding-100`) — a one-time $139 lifetime Pro tier,
  capped at 100 spots, with a live checkout. The cap is a real business-health
  guardrail (lifetime plans are genuinely risky past a point), stated as such.
  We push it because it's a good deal for the right people, not because we
  fake a countdown.

Post-launch, the newsletter CTAs stay; the signup CTAs are already live. See
`docs/PUBLIC-PAGES.md` for the full public-surface spec.
