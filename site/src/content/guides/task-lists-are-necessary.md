A task list is not the problem. It is where you put promises so your brain does not have to keep rehearsing them.

The trouble starts when the full inventory becomes the screen you work from. A list of fifty tasks is useful when you are reviewing commitments. It is a terrible surface for deciding what to do at 10:17 on a Tuesday. Every visible task asks for attention. Most do not deserve it yet.

## Keep two different surfaces

You need an **accountability surface**: the complete, trusted record. It holds tasks, projects, ideas for later, and the things you have consciously decided not to do.

You also need an **action surface**: a deliberately small view of work that can move now.

These are not competing systems. They are two jobs. The first answers, “Have I forgotten anything important?” The second answers, “What am I doing next?”

## Let the full list appear at the right time

Open the whole system when you capture, triage, plan, or review. Those are decision-making moments. Do not open it every time you want to begin work.

When you sit down to act, start with one next task. If it is wrong, switch deliberately. If it is right, do not reopen the argument just because forty-nine other tasks still exist.

The aim is not to hide commitments from yourself. It is to stop confusing “I need to remember this” with “I need to look at this right now.”
