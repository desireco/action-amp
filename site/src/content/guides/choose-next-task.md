When everything feels important, a cleverer ranking system rarely saves you. The problem is not that you have failed to assign enough numbers. It is that too many options are asking to be chosen at once.

Ask a smaller question: **what can I honestly move forward in the time and state I have?**

## The ten-minute reset

1. Name reality first: time available, energy, and a real deadline today.
2. Ignore tasks that cannot move in this window. A two-hour task is not next when you have twenty minutes.
3. Prefer a task with a consequence if it waits, or one that unblocks someone else.
4. Rewrite it as a visible first action. “Plan launch” becomes “open launch brief and write three audience questions.”
5. Put everything else out of sight until this action is finished or deliberately stopped.

If two tasks still tie, choose the smaller one. Momentum is information: finishing it may clarify the larger decision.

## Keep a reason beside the task

Write one sentence: “I am doing this because…” A deadline, a promise, a dependency, or a small win all count. If you cannot name a reason, it probably belongs in later, not now.

The goal is not perfect prioritization. It is an honest next move.
